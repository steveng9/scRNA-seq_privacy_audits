## Reviewer kPMB  (draft)

We thank the reviewer for a careful, detailed review and respond to each point below; several are resolved by re-analysis of saved results, and two by new experiments (disjoint-cell membership; a marginal-noise defense).

**W1 / Q1: Auxiliary-data contamination. [AC-1]** Our sampler draws D_aux from non-target donors first, so D_aux is disjoint from the target set (train ∪ holdout) by construction. We audited every split used in the paper: D_aux is provably disjoint from the target set in 100% of trials across all datasets, except at the largest donor size per dataset (OneK1K 490, AIDA 200, HFRA ≥10), where the limited donor pool forces some overlap — and only the "+aux" threat model is affected there, never "−aux". We corrected each. For OneK1K we re-ran the large-cohort case as a balanced 440-donor split with a fully-disjoint aux (5 trials): the attack is unchanged — BB+aux 0.53 AUC — so overlap inflated nothing (near chance at this size regardless of aux). For AIDA-200 we re-ran with a smaller, disjoint aux and the numbers match within noise (standard BB+aux 0.77 → 0.80, WB+aux 0.87 → 0.92 — so overlap did not inflate anything). HFRA's pool cannot supply a disjoint aux at these sizes, so we drop its two "+aux" points as a small-pool limitation. Corrected numbers go into the camera-ready.

**W2: Reliance on the donor's exact training cells. [AC-5]** Two controlled analyses. (i) *Cells-per-donor.* Re-aggregating the standard BB+aux attack over only k ∈ {1, 10, 100} cells per donor (with member and non-member donors capped at the same k and cells sampled uniformly within each donor), **the attack is already above chance from a single cell** and sharpens with k, but sub-proportionally to the cell count: donors contribute ≈1,300 cells on average in OneK1K and ≈2,000 in AIDA, so k=100 is under 10% of a donor's cells yet recovers most of the signal. The signal is therefore per-cell; more cells only sharpen the donor-level estimate rather than creating the effect (and a donor's cell count and membership score are only weakly, even slightly negatively, correlated — mean r ≈ −0.1 across cohorts — so the most-sampled donors are no easier, to identify). Standard BB+aux, 5-trial means:

| dataset / cohort | k=1 | k=10 | k=100 | all |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OneK1K 20d  | 0.54 | 0.59 | 0.67 | 0.69 |
| OneK1K 50d  | 0.56 | 0.57 | 0.65 | 0.65 |
| OneK1K 100d | 0.50 | 0.54 | 0.58 | 0.57 |
| OneK1K 200d | 0.53 | 0.54 | 0.55 | 0.55 |
| AIDA 10d    | 0.62 | 0.87 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| AIDA 100d   | 0.58 | 0.70 | 0.87 | 0.91 |

(ii) *Equal-budget, disjoint-cell experiment (new; 5 trials).* For each member donor the generator is trained on 200 cells, while a **disjoint** 200 cells from that same donor (never seen by the generator) are used as the attack targets; an "overlap" control instead targets the 200 cells the generator was trained on. The striking result is that membership is still detectable from the disjoint cells: a donor's held-out cells reveal their presence in a training set that shares none of them, because cells from one donor are more mutually similar than cells across donors and the copula fingerprint captures this. The disjoint signal is high in small cohorts and decays toward, but stays above, chance as the cohort grows, while overlap stays high throughout. Enhanced BB+aux attack, 5-trial means; columns are cohort sizes (OneK1K, AIDA, HFRA are three distinct datasets):

The gap holds for the non-enhanced attack and without auxiliary data too (50d, overlap / disjoint): standard BB+aux OneK1K 0.81 / 0.59 and AIDA 1.00 / 0.56; standard BB−aux OneK1K 0.73 / 0.58 and AIDA 0.92 / 0.49. So the paper's setting is an upper bound, but the vulnerability persists when the attacker never observes the training cells; it is most severe in the realistic small-cohort regime (e.g. rare-disease atlases). We will add these tables with ±1 std. **The same disjoint > chance pattern also holds on scVI**, a non-copula neural generator, so the finding is not specific to scDesign2 (details in our reply to reviewer JvKY, W4).

These are two questions specific to membership inference on single-cell data, and we are grateful to have been prompted to add the analysis.

**W3 / Q3: Attack details. [AC-4]** (a) *Discrete PIT hitting 0/1:* counts pass through the fitted marginal CDF and the resulting uniforms are clipped inside (0,1) before Φ⁻¹, so the transform is never infinite (scDesign2's own distributional-transform convention). (b) *Singular covariance:* we invert with the pseudo-inverse (plus a PSD projection when the matrix is noised), so genes > cells is handled without failure. (c) *Copula dimension differing across cell types:* distances are never mixed across cell types; each is computed in its own copula space and converted to a per-cell score before per-donor averaging (already in Sec. 3.2). We will make (a)–(c) explicit in the methods.

**W4 / Q5: Only AUC; scores aren't probabilities; trials; no DP baseline; originality. [AC-2]** (a) *Metrics beyond AUC:* from saved scores we add TPR at low FPR and precision under a realistic prior, standard BB+aux attack, donor-level: AIDA 10d TPR@1%FPR = 1.00; OneK1K 20d TPR@10%FPR = 0.28 (precision 0.24 at a 10% membership prior, vs 0.10 base rate); above the false-positive line even at OneK1K 200d (0.12 @ 10%FPR). The low-FPR view reinforces the attack in small cohorts; we will add these tables. (b) *Scores:* correct; these are calibrated ranking statistics, not probabilities; we will relabel them "membership scores." (c) *No DP baseline:* to our knowledge no differentially private scRNA-seq generator exists to compare against; the nearest works are DP single-cell *clustering* (DP-DCAN), DP+MPC pipelines for *bulk* RNA-seq, and scDesignPop (a 2026 population-scale copula generator) that defends eQTL-based *re-identification via linking*, a threat orthogonal to membership, with no formal DP. This absence is part of our motivation. (d) *Originality:* scMAMA-MIA adapts the MAMA-MIA *heuristic*, but as a concrete attack it is new: the first to run above chance on synthetic **single-cell genomic** data, a regime where general tabular MIAs typically do not beat random guessing, via a copula-Mahalanobis statistic these methods lack. Since submission, it won the **ISMB CAMDA 2026 Health Privacy Challenge (single-cell track)** (an independent, third-party evaluation of our exact attack). (e) *Trials:* we report ±1 std over 5 donor splits throughout.

**W5 / Q4: Defense evaluated only vs the basic attack, and not the marginals. [AC-6]** The reviewer is right; space allowed only one noise target in the paper, so we now compare two: noise on the covariance (the paper's) versus noise on the per-gene marginals. Since a raw noise magnitude measures different physical perturbations for the two targets and is not comparable, we read each along the **privacy–utility frontier**: sweeping the noise (OneK1K and AIDA, 50 donors, 5 trials, enhanced attack), at each achieved AUC we report the fidelity each mechanism must pay to reach it. The metrics are complementary: **LISI** = how well real and synthetic cells intermix (integration; ↑ good, ≈0 = separable), **ARI** = whether the data still clusters by cell type (biological structure), **MMD** = global distributional distance in PCA space (↓ good). OneK1K, enhanced BB+aux:

| enhanced-attack AUC | covariance noise: LISI / ARI / MMD×10³ | marginal noise: LISI / ARI / MMD×10³ |
|---|---|---|
| 0.79 (no noise) | 0.87 / 0.47 / 0.4 | 0.87 / 0.47 / 0.4 |
| 0.73 | 0.70 / 0.48 / 0.5 | 0.68 / 0.49 / 4.7 |
| 0.69 | 0.66 / 0.49 / 0.5  ← **covariance floor** | 0.55 / 0.49 / 6.5 |
| 0.60 | *unreachable* | 0.01 / 0.48 / 30 |
| 0.54 | *unreachable* | 0.00 / 0.47 / 101 |

Two findings. (1) **Covariance noise has a floor it cannot cross.** No covariance noise pushes the enhanced attack below AUC ≈0.69 on OneK1K (≈0.73 on AIDA): its evidence lives in the per-gene marginals, which covariance noise never touches. It *does* defeat the basic (covariance-only) attack (→≈0.50) at very low fidelity cost (ARI and MMD stay flat, only LISI dips), so where covariance leakage is the operative concern it is an attractive, utility-preserving option — but against the marginals-aware attack it stalls at ≈0.69, so the paper's apparent "crossover" reflects testing only the basic attack. (2) **Only marginal noise lowers the strong attack, at a steep, uneven fidelity cost.** Reaching AUC ≈0.6 already collapses LISI to ≈0 and inflates MMD ~50×; the floor of ≈0.54 on OneK1K (≈0.52 on AIDA — still marginally above chance) needs MMD ≈200×. Marginal noise degrades even the covariance-based signal, because both attacks compute their distance in marginal-CDF coordinates — corrupting the marginals corrupts that coordinate system even with the covariance intact — so separately noising the covariance adds essentially nothing, and we omit it. Throughout, ARI barely moves: the fidelity failure is specific (integration and global distribution), not uniform blurring. Net: no single-parameter noise defends the strong attack at usable fidelity, matching the AC's view that a rigorous DP mechanism is a separate contribution.

**Limitations / responsible disclosure.** We will expand the limitations (the all-cells assumption, now tested by the disjoint-cell experiment; small-pool datasets like HFRA) and add a responsible-disclosure statement.

## Reviewer oT6m  (draft)

We thank the reviewer for a detailed, confident review, and address each point.

**scDiffusion MMD ("45.69" vs [25]'s "0.32").** These are not comparable quantities and there is no inconsistency. Our MMD is computed between real and synthetic data in PCA space on our datasets and is reported **scaled by 10³** (header "MMD×10³"), so "45.69" is 0.0457 in absolute terms; it is also a different dataset and preprocessing than [25]. The row is comparative *within our table*: scDiffusion's fidelity is far below the copula/VAE methods, which is why its attack AUC sits near 0.5; its resistance reflects **low fidelity, not privacy protection**, the interpretive principle the paper emphasizes. We will annotate the scaling in the caption.

**Why scDesign2 is the attack's shadow model; ablation of that choice. [AC-2/3]** scDesign2 has the highest fidelity under the CAMDA2025 metrics, and its Gaussian copula is exactly the structure the Mahalanobis statistic exploits, so we fit it as the black-box proxy even when attacking other generators; scDesign3 in default (Gaussian) mode shares that copula and gives near-identical proxy results. The attack transfers across scDesign3 (Gaussian and Vine copula variants), scVI, and ZINB-WaVE via this single proxy (cross-model table in the manuscript, Tables 2, 4, 7, 8). We also ran a systematic ablation (25 scDesign2 trials, 3 datasets) of every attack component. Components studied: the marginal CDF used for the copula PIT; the covariance-inverse method (pseudo-inverse vs direct); the nearest-PSD correlation projection; the copula gene set for the Mahalanobis term; per-cell-type vs pooled copula spaces; the cell→donor aggregation (inverse-distance, sigmoid, mean); the auxiliary-reference ratio λ = d_a/(d_s+d_a); and, for the enhanced term, the scoring rule, its gene set, and its weight γ. Key results: the decisive enhancement is a per-gene likelihood-ratio evaluated on the *secondary genes* — the genes scDesign2 fits with marginals only and deliberately leaves out of its copula covariance (BB+aux 0.72 → 0.83). Restricting that term to those secondary genes beats using all genes (0.83 vs 0.79), the auto-normalized weight γ works best, and a diagonal-Mahalanobis scoring variant adds nothing (≈ baseline). We will add the full ablation table and discussion in the appendix.

**Report TPR at low FPR (Carlini et al.), not just AUC. [AC-2]** Done from saved per-cell scores (standard BB+aux, donor-level), in alignment with [Carlini et al.]: AIDA 10d TPR@1%FPR = 1.00; OneK1K 20d TPR@10%FPR = 0.28 (precision 0.24 at a 10% prior); above the false-positive line even at OneK1K 200d (0.12 @ 10%FPR). This conclusively shows severe small-cohort leakage and clearly above-chance signal at scale. We will add complete TPR-at-FPR tables accompanying the headline results, at FPR ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20}.

**Auxiliary-data justification / possible test-set contamination. [AC-1]** We built the sampler to draw D_aux from non-target donors first, so aux is disjoint from the target set by design; auditing every saved split confirms it: fully disjoint in 100% of OneK1K trials at every donor count in the main sweep (≤200) and all AIDA trials ≤100 donors, with overlap forced only where the donor pool is exhausted (490-donor OneK1K, 22 HFRA; 200 AIDA). We re-ran those few affected +aux configs with a disjoint aux and the numbers match the reported values (figures in our reply to kPMB W1). The headline "+aux" results are sound, and no "−aux" result is affected.

**"Best AUC at 2 donors, dropping sharply with more donors" and "sub-0.5 = wrong direction."** The decline with cohort size is the paper's central finding, not a defect: with fewer donors each donor more strongly shapes the copula, so leakage is stronger; as the cohort grows individuals "hide among the crowd", precisely the privacy warning for rare-disease / small cohorts, which we state in the conclusion. The decline is smooth and monotone rather than a "sharp" drop: at more than double the paper's largest cohort (OneK1K, 440 training donors, 5 trials) the black-box attack has settled at chance (BB+aux AUC 0.52 standard, 0.56 enhanced) while the stronger white-box attack retains only a small residual edge (WB+aux 0.60) — the expected dilution of a per-donor signal, not an erratic artifact. The sub-0.5 entries occur only in the −aux columns of the enhanced attack: its likelihood-ratio term is a contrast between synthetic and auxiliary fits, so without auxiliary data it has no reference and is disabled by design (weight 0); the sub-0.5 values are that uncalibrated term at small n, not a reversed predictor; the standard −aux attack behaves correctly. We will clarify this in the algorithm description. (We believe the reviewer's "Table 4" here is meant to reference Table 6, which reports the enhanced −aux results where these sub-0.5 entries appear; we will make that table's caption unambiguous.)

## Reviewer JvKY  (draft)

We thank the reviewer for a constructive review, and address each point.

**W1 / Q1: More principled defense baselines. [AC-6]** To our knowledge there is no differentially private scRNA-seq generator to use as a baseline: existing single-cell DP work does *clustering* not generation (DP-DCAN), DP+MPC pipelines target *bulk* RNA-seq, and the newest privacy-oriented single-cell generator (scDesignPop, a 2026 population-scale copula method) defends eQTL-based *re-identification via linking* (orthogonal to membership inference) with no formal DP, and being copula-based it would plausibly remain vulnerable to our attack. We therefore compare against the strongest available synthetic-data MIAs (LOGAN, GAN-Leaks, DOMIAS, MC) and, as a defense, compare noise on the covariance versus on the marginals, traced along the privacy–utility frontier (full analysis in our reply to kPMB W5): covariance noise defeats the basic attack cheaply but cannot push the enhanced attack below ≈0.69 at any fidelity, so marginal noise is the only lever that goes lower — and even that reaches only ≈0.54 (at 50 donors) at the cost of destroyed fidelity. This is direct evidence for the paper's thesis, and, as the AC notes, a rigorous DP defense is a separate contribution.

**W2 / Q2: Systematic ablation; comparison to MAMA-MIA; white-box.** (a) We ran a 25-trial ablation isolating every attack component (full list in our reply to oT6m; the complete table will go in the appendix); the marginal likelihood-ratio on secondary genes is the dominant enhancement (BB+aux 0.72 → 0.83). (b) MAMA-MIA is a *heuristic*, not a fixed attack, and every existing implementation targets small, non-genomic tabular data. We ported one directly (a PrivBayes-based variant: it learns low-order gene relationships from reference data and scores each donor by how much better the synthetic data fits those relationships than the reference does) and ran it on scDesign2 (OneK1K, 5 trials). Like the general tabular MIAs we already include (LOGAN, GAN-Leaks, DOMIAS, MC, all near 0.5 at genomic scale), it does not transfer: at 10 donors it reaches only AUC 0.54±0.10 (TPR 0.20 at 10% FPR, noteably above the 0.10 chance rate) and falls to chance as the cohort grows (50d 0.50, 200d 0.49), even though the generator's training data grows from ~12k cells at 10 donors to ~258k at 200. Making an attack succeed in this regime at all is the contribution. (c) We report white-box results (WB±aux) across donor sizes and will surface them more prominently. We note two scoping points: white-box leakage is higher (as the paper shows), but the setting applies only to scDesign2 — it assumes direct access to the fitted copula, which the other generators do not expose — and is a deliberately strong, arguably less realistic threat model; it was not used in the CAMDA 2025/2026 challenge, so we treat black-box as our primary setting.
