DEFENSE BASELINES: EARLY STOPPING / DROPOUT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We apologize for the misunderstanding: from the original review's phrasing
("stronger or more principled privacy-preserving baselines where applicable") we
did not realize that early stopping and dropout were the specific baselines
intended, and we are grateful for the clarification. We are aware of this line of
work — that membership leakage is driven largely by overfitting, and that
early stopping, dropout, and related regularization are the standard,
low-cost mitigations (Shokri et al., IEEE S&P 2017; Nasr et al., CCS 2018;
Salem et al. "ML-Leaks", NDSS 2019, which evaluates dropout and model stacking
directly as membership-inference defenses).

Applicability. Early stopping and dropout are defenses of an iterative,
gradient-trained model: they require training epochs, a validation signal, and/or
dropout layers. They therefore do not apply to the statistical generators that
are the focus of this work — scDesign2 (our primary target), scDesign3 (both the
Gaussian- and vine-copula variants we study), ZINB-WaVE, and NMF. These fit
closed-form / EM marginal and copula parameters; there are no epochs to stop, no
minibatch SGD, and no dropout layers, so there is no such knob to set. In short,
the defenses named apply to exactly two of our six generators, and not to the
primary target.

A brief clarification of our previous reply, to be sure it was not lost: the
covariance-noise defense is the one from the original submission; the
marginal-noise defense we reported alongside it in our rebuttal is new for this
review and is a substantially stronger line of defense than the covariance noise
we had previously studied. So we have in fact taken more extreme measures to
defend scDesign2 against our attack since the original submission, and the
covariance-vs-marginal comparison is a real extension, not a restatement.

The two neural generators (scVI, scDiffusion). Here early stopping and dropout do
apply, and we already use them:
  - scVI is trained with dropout (rate 0.1) and early stopping (validation-based,
    patience 20) enabled — i.e. the exact regularizers suggested are already
    active in every scVI result we report, and the attack still transfers well
    (enhanced BB+aux around 0.8-0.85 at small cohorts, e.g. 0.81 on OneK1K-10d,
    0.85 on AIDA-10d).
  - scDiffusion is trained with the original implementation's default
    hyperparameters and schedule (including dropout in its guidance classifier).
    Its synthetic fidelity is already low (Section/Table [X]; MMD far above the
    copula and VAE methods), so its attack AUC already sits near chance not
    because it resists membership inference but because it is not modeling the
    data closely enough to memorize it — the same "resistance tracks low fidelity,
    not privacy protection" principle we emphasize throughout. Further
    regularization here would only lower fidelity further, moving down the same
    privacy-utility frontier rather than providing privacy for free.

For both neural models we use the original implementations' default
(hyper)parameters; a broader hyperparameter search to tune these NN-specific
defenses further is out of scope for this work, whose contribution and primary
targets are the copula-based generators to which these defenses do not apply.
